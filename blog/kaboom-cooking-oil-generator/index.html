The Kaboom story: mining bitcoin with used cooking oil

In 2022 the Bitcoin Lago team at Lake Atitlán ran an old diesel generator on used restaurant oil to power bitcoin miners. Here is how it started, what the sources say it earned, and what we still cannot confirm.

Written by the Bitcoin Lago site team from the sources linked in each paragraph. 22 Oct 2026.

What Kaboom is

Kaboom is the used cooking oil project of Bitcoin Lago in Panajachel. An old diesel generator burns oil from nearby restaurants instead of diesel. The electricity it makes runs bitcoin miners, with no link to the power grid. A miner is a computer built for one job: it makes guesses very fast, which is called hashing, to win the right to add the next block. A block is one page of bitcoin's shared record, and the winner earns new bitcoin. Patrick Melder told Bitcoin Magazine in September 2022 that Kaboom came from a wish to help clean Lake Atitlán while giving the community a steady income. [Bitcoin Eco. podcast, 30:34; One Love Bitcoin, 36:35; Bitcoin Magazine, Sept 2022]

Why oil? The electricity bill

Eliazar says mining began in March, with an S9 miner at the school. The S9 is a less efficient model; Patrick said so in March 2022. Grid power in Guatemala cost about 17 or 20 cents per kilowatt hour, Eliazar says. The host answers that profitable miners pay 5 to 7 cents. Patrick gave a similar figure of 20 or 22 cents in March 2022. At those prices, Eliazar says, "now we can't do this." So the team looked for cheaper power. [One Love Bitcoin, 26:26; One Love Bitcoin, 27:08; Patrick Melder, Mar 2022, 18:36; Patrick Melder, Mar 2022, 22:05]

Bill's idea and an old generator

Eliazar credits his mining partner, Bill Whittaker, with the idea. Around June or July, most likely 2022, Bill pointed out that a diesel engine can run on vegetable oil. They went looking for a generator in what Eliazar's captions call "chimal tenango", probably Chimaltenango, a town known for vehicle parts. They walked all day and found a plain generator on the ground in something like a junkyard. Eliazar says it was 50 to 60 years old, but it worked on diesel. Bill bought it. They had no truck that day, so they came back for it. [One Love Bitcoin, 27:57; One Love Bitcoin, 28:34; One Love Bitcoin, 29:50; One Love Bitcoin, 30:13]

Filtering the oil through jeans

Next they asked for used cooking oil on the main street and got about two gallons. They did not know how to filter it. Filtering means passing the oil through cloth so that the solid bits stay behind. Bill called a friend, and the advice was to use jeans, like Levi's. Eliazar brought his own pair the next day. They started the engine on diesel, then switched to the oil. The only difference, Eliazar says, is the smell. [One Love Bitcoin, 31:21; One Love Bitcoin, 31:58; One Love Bitcoin, 32:27]

A loud engine and a name

Nobody wanted a loud engine nearby. Eliazar says Bill and a friend found a small parking place that worked as a mechanic's space near the river and rented it. When Bill saw it, he said Kaboom, and the name stuck. A video the team posted went viral on the Lago Bitcoin account on X. The host says three machines were running. Eliazar also says they first turned the engine on "right there in the school", so the order of events is not fully clear. [One Love Bitcoin, 34:07; One Love Bitcoin, 36:08; One Love Bitcoin, 32:16]

What it earned, and who paid

The Get Based film, probably recorded in late 2022, says Kaboom makes about 230 dollars of bitcoin a month, about half an average monthly salary in Guatemala. That figure comes from the narrator, not from Eliazar. An April 2024 post by the video maker Adam O'Brien also says Kaboom can generate half of a monthly salary. We have no figure for what the oil, rent and repairs cost. In October 2022 the project asked for help for Kaboom through a fundraising page, and in November for small donations to pay its rent and internet. Eliazar says the project lives only on donations. [Get Based, 3:47; Adam O'Brien on X, Apr 2024; Lago Bitcoin on X, Oct 2022; Lago Bitcoin on X, Nov 2022; One Love Bitcoin, 33:42]

A classroom and a tour stop

In the Get Based film Eliazar says the oil used to go to the garbage, the rivers or the lake. On a May 2023 podcast the guests said children from Centro Educativo Josué were taken to see how it worked. At Adopting Bitcoin in November 2023 the team said so too, and added that a volunteer with a mining background helped explain it. Visitors came too: a German TV crew, a demo at the Lago Summit in May 2023, and the Get Based crew. [Get Based, 3:08; Bitcoin Eco. podcast, 31:38; Adopting Bitcoin 2023, 9:23; German TV post, Mar 2023; Lago Summit post, May 2023]

Does it still run?

In May 2023 the English speaker, Patrick by our notes, said: "Now we can't run it efficiently because of economies of scale." That means one small engine does not pay off well. The Spanish part of the answer says the project is still there, as a proof of concept and a stop for bitcoin tourists. In February 2024 Eliazar posted that they had more oil to run Kaboom and that he was fixing its engine. In October 2024 he posted "Plastic to Diesel" with Kaboom, and Bill wrote that Kaboom ran on diesel made from waste plastic. We found no 2025 or 2026 interview that mentions Kaboom. A January 2026 repost mentions Kaboom mining at the lake, but it is someone else's cut-off post, so we do not say it runs today. [Bitcoin Eco. podcast, 30:57; Bitcoin Eco. podcast, 31:15; Eliazar on X, 6 Feb 2024; Eliazar on X, 6 Feb 2024 (engine); Eliazar on X, Oct 2024; Bill Whittaker on X, Oct 2024; Repost on X, Jan 2026]

Not the town hall miner, not the biogas plan

Two other mining stories sit close to Kaboom. On March 25, 2022, the Panajachel mayor accepted a first donated miner, and a July 2022 post says the town hall S9 had been hashing since that day. In March 2022 Patrick described a plan to fix the cracked biodigester at the town's wastewater plant and burn its methane to run miners, which Cointelegraph covered in April 2022 as a biogas plan. Both are different from Kaboom, which burns used cooking oil in a generator. [Mayor accepts miner, 25 Mar 2022; Town hall S9 post, Jul 2022; Patrick Melder, Mar 2022, 16:58; Cointelegraph, Apr 2022]

The difference is that you start smelling like french fries.
