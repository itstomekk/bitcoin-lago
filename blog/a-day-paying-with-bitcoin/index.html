A day paying only with bitcoin: what visitors found, 2022 to 2025

Visitors have managed full days on bitcoin around Panajachel, but their reports also show outdated listings, payment hiccups and the need for cash backup. Here is what worked, and what did not.

Written by the Bitcoin Lago site team from the sources linked in each paragraph. 23 Oct 2026.

Can you spend a whole day around Panajachel using only bitcoin? The visitor videos from 2022 to 2025 give a practical answer: sometimes, with a little flexibility. One traveler went eight hours without reaching for cash or a card; another tried several listed places and found that most could not process a payment. These are individual trips, not a promise that every business on a map is ready today. [Tekoa Spaces, 8:28; The Orange Future, 6:43]

2022: optimism, with a cash warning

In December 2022, the Bitcoin Explorers filmed a trip through Antigua and Panajachel. They described finding bitcoin acceptance across restaurants, travel agencies and local transport, including tuk-tuks and boats. But they also reported no bitcoin ATM around the lake and advised changing bitcoin for quetzales before arriving if cash might be needed. Their Italian-language companion episode, published the same day, recounts the same journey, so it is one visit, not two independent tests. Adam O'Brien posted a nearly silent teaser from a quick Panajachel trip that month; its description mentions Lightning payments, but it does not document a merchant or a purchase, so it adds little detail about what worked. [Bitcoin Explorers, 9:18; Bitcoin Explorers, 10:04; Italian companion episode, 8:20; Adam O'Brien video description]

2023: real payments, and real growing pains

On the Sunday before Pizza Day in May 2023, a visitor from Reverend Hodl tried three places for breakfast and coffee. One accepted bitcoin. A lakeside cafe shown on the Bitcoin map no longer did, so he said he would alert the group to update the listing. At the place that did accept it, he paid for breakfast over Lightning with Wallet of Satoshi to the merchant's Bitcoin Beach wallet, now called Blink. He described the payment as instant and noted that the app offered a tip prompt. The day showed both sides of adoption: a payment can be quick once the staff and wallet are ready, while a listing can outlast a merchant's willingness to accept bitcoin. [Reverend Hodl, 2:14; Reverend Hodl, 4:28; Reverend Hodl, 7:18]

At a Pizza Day gathering the next day, visitors saw the community's first bitcoin-only ATM being unveiled. It was not dispensing yet: the video says an on-chain purchase was possible, while Lightning buying and selling were expected later that week. That is a dated report of the machine's launch, not a statement about current availability. A separate Tekoa Spaces video offers a more upbeat snapshot: a group toured Panajachel, paid at breakfast, coffee stops, lunch and dinner with Lightning, and tipped in bitcoin. They also helped a barbershop set up payments that day, testing transactions with the owner. The video says the group spent hours without using cash or a card, but it is one organized tour, not proof every visitor can repeat the route without preparation. [Reverend Hodl, 3:24; Tekoa Spaces, 1:38; Tekoa Spaces, 4:22; Tekoa Spaces, 8:28]

2025: workarounds, uneven acceptance

In early 2025, Paco De La India could not get online on his phone at a cafe. Eliazar's brother sent him a QR invoice, and Paco paid from his own wallet. The payment succeeded, but the workaround depended on someone nearby who could help. Paco later found the bitcoin ATM down. His trip also included a boat ride to San Pedro quoted at 25 quetzales; the footage does not establish that he paid that fare in bitcoin, so do not count it as a bitcoin purchase. His advice was to support local businesses, while recognizing that handling bitcoin can be difficult for merchants. [Paco De La India, 3:07; Paco De La India, 8:02; Paco De La India, 8:34]

The Orange Future, visiting in February 2025, had a less smooth week. The traveler estimated that only about a quarter of the places he tried could actually process his payment. He describes missing devices and a language barrier, then having to return to an ATM for cash. That figure is his experience, not a controlled survey, and should not be treated as the success rate for every merchant. Still, it is an important counterweight to the phrase bitcoin-only: a sign, a map entry or a past successful payment does not guarantee the next one. [The Orange Future, 6:13; The Orange Future, 6:43; The Orange Future, 7:15]

A repeatable route, not a current guarantee

For a route grounded in more than one visit, start with 80's Cafe, Chinajul, Amaranto and Delhi 6: Tekoa Spaces included them in its May 2023 bitcoin tour, and The Orange Future reported successful payments at the same four places in February 2025. The Orange Future said Delhi 6 accepted bitcoin about half the time, so even this repeat list is not a guarantee. Treat it as a historically tested route to ask about, not a live directory. The Tekoa description also includes Coffee Art for breakfast in 2023; that visit is not repeated in the 2025 report, so it has just one source here. [Tekoa Spaces description and route, 5:32; The Orange Future, 7:15; The Orange Future, 8:48]

Bitrefill's March 2023 video with Eliazar names boats, water bicycles, jet skis and paragliding as activities that could be paid for with bitcoin at that time. It names no operator and gives no prices. Consider those historical examples, not confirmed current offers, and ask the provider before making plans. [Bitrefill, 1:35]

Before you set out

Ask before ordering, even if you see a bitcoin sign or find the place on a map. Carry some quetzales for a failed or unavailable payment, and have a backup wallet plus mobile data if you can: the videos show both failed devices and a visitor whose phone had no internet. If service is poor, ask whether staff can send a QR invoice another way, but do not assume someone will be available to help. Tip in sats when it suits you; the 2023 tour paid a bitcoin tip, and the Reverend Hodl visitor saw a tip prompt. If a mapped place is closed or no longer accepts bitcoin, report the change on BTC Map so the next visitor can check more reliable information. Spending helps keep the local circular economy moving, but the honest plan is bitcoin first, with a backup. [Bitcoin Explorers, 12:22; Bitcoin Explorers, 2:27; Paco De La India, 3:07; Tekoa Spaces, 1:38; Reverend Hodl, 7:18; Reverend Hodl, 2:48; Bitcoin Lago merchant story, 7:04]
